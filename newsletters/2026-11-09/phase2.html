This Week — Nov 3–9, 2026
RBI reported Q3 on Thu Oct 29, still its latest hard news (nothing new from the company surfaced in the Nov 3–9 scan). Burger King U.S. "same-store sales" (sales at restaurants open a year or more) rose 8.4%, and system-wide sales rose 7.5%. Across all four brands, same-store sales rose 3.8% and adjusted earnings were $1.10 a share, 2 cents above the analyst guess. Revenue of $2.53B was about $20M short of the guess.
The brands are not moving together. Popeyes same-store sales fell 1.8%, and management no longer expects it to turn positive in the second half of 2026. Tim Hortons Canada rose 1.8%. International same-store sales rose 3.9%. A news headline noted the stock slipped after the report despite the Burger King news.
Money: free cash flow was over $500M and RBI returned $569M to shareholders in the quarter. Net leverage (debt compared with yearly earnings) ended at 4.0x, and RBI wants low-to-mid 3x by 2028. The board kept the quarterly dividend at $0.65 per share. A finance team paying down debt wants proof that spending saves money.
The "Royal Reset" plan (up to $550M for Burger King U.S. remodels and kitchen equipment through 2028) is the funded program to sell into; remodels moved slowly this quarter (Finsee summary of the call). Burger King China is not new news: RBI sold about 83% to CPE and the deal closed on Feb 2, 2026. RBI keeps 17% and a board seat.
Next report is expected about mid-February 2027 (last year's Q4 came Feb 12). The date is not confirmed.

On Oct 2 (38 days ago) the FTC filed a proposed settlement with Southern Glazer's. It is the first Robinson-Patman win in 26 years. That law bans charging a small store more than a big chain for the same goods.
The rules are specific. For six years, in 26 states, the order watches pairs of sales: a chain buys a product, and within 45 days (75 for some chains) a nearby independent store buys a similar one at a much higher price. "Nearby" means 1.5 to 2.5 miles in cities, 6 miles in suburbs and 12 miles in rural areas. Twice a year, SGWS must hand its sales data to an independent monitor, and SGWS pays for the monitor.
If the monitor finds an overcharge, SGWS can fix it by paying the store 1.5 times the price gap. If it does not fix it and the FTC has to go to court, the payment goes to 2 times. That means SGWS has to check every pair of sales, all the time.
