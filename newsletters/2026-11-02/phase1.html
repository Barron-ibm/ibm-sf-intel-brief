For six years, in 26 states, the order watches pairs of sales: a chain buys a product, and within 45 days (75 for some chains) a nearby independent store buys a similar one at a much higher price. "Nearby" means 1.5 to 2.5 miles in cities, 6 miles in suburbs and 12 miles in rural areas. Twice a year, SGWS must hand its sales data to an independent monitor, and SGWS pays for the monitor.
  • If the monitor finds an overcharge, SGWS can fix it by paying the store 1.5 times the price gap. If it does not fix it and the FTC has to go to court, the payment goes to 2 times. That means SGWS has to check every pair of sales, all the time.
